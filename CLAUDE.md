# tang.ninja

Yang Tang's research homepage plus his kids' browser games. Static site on Cloudflare Workers (static assets + a tiny router), built by `node build.mjs` (no dependencies) into `dist/` on every push and deployed with `npx wrangler deploy` (`wrangler.jsonc`). `dist/` is not committed. The user's own guide is README.md (Chinese); keep it accurate when the workflow changes.

## Layout
- `home/index.html`: tang.ninja, research and CV (Bauhaus + Apple look). An illustrated hero (title top left, numbered section index top right), then a white sheet with sections 01 to 06 matching that index. The build fills `<!-- SCENE -->` with the hero illustration. Everything else in `home/` (home.js, hero/, images) is copied to the site root.
- `home/hero/`: the hero illustration, all drawn by `python3 tools/hero.py`: one SVG per depth layer on a shared 1600x720 canvas (sky, clouds, far city, monster, mid city with the water, robot, near buildings, pier, a close foreground), small animated pieces cut from that canvas, lit-window overlays, and `scene.html`, the markup that places them all (data-depth, positions in %). Edit the script and rerun it, never the SVGs or scene.html by hand. The characters and vehicles are flat colour with a bold ink outline (`Inked` in the script), no highlights or rim light (the user's choice: Bauhaus, no light and shade). The monster is the red block of a cyclops (the user's favourite of the versions tried; a dopey dinosaur was tried and dropped); the tower between the giants carries a clock (a red circle there looked odd to the user); the robot's two arms and two legs share one design. No magic seal (dropped). Also in the picture: a forged sword with pulsing runes in the robot's left fist, jets in a V, tanks on the quay, a news helicopter, a ferry and a tug, a ferris wheel, a smith's forge on a right rooftop, an original fastback coupe (not a real make: the user asked for a Porsche 911, which is not drawn) on the pier, and in the close foreground a bollard with a gull and a journalist filming from a tripod. Easter eggs: a rubber duck (in open water right of the robot, where no higher layer covers its box), a periscope, a UFO behind the cloud, a cat in a window, the rabbit in the moon. No aliens: the user asked for them, then had all of them removed. The user asked why the site can't carry the IPs he loves since it isn't commercial; the answer given: copyright doesn't turn on commercial use, the page is public, so the characters stay original.
- Motion (all asked for by the user): the ferry, the tug, the car and the jets cross the screen one way at constant speed and come round again (`fx-cross`, linear, `--x0/--x1`); the tanks stop and go, each its own distance and pace (`fx-patrol`); the helicopter (facing left) rises and sinks a good way (`fx-lift`); the drones swing about the robot with their camera frustums (`fx-swing` about a pivot) and bob; the satellite passes every 13 s on its orbit from off the left to off the right, at constant speed, while its beam turns and stretches to stay on the monster (`orbit-sat`/`orbit-beam` keyframes, worked out from the geometry by the script and written into scene.html's `<style>`); the ferris wheel turns; the clock on the tower shows the visitor's local time (three hand pieces, `.clock-h/m/s`, turned by home.js every second); the crane hook sways; the TV camera pans; the monster blinks; the robot's visor sweeps; the periscope sinks and rises; the UFO darts. The robot's punching arm is its own piece (`arm.svg`, `fx-punch`) striking every 2.4 s with the burst, flash and sparks repeating. `Layer.mover()` groups a piece, its effects and its detection box in one animated div; paths are given in canvas px and written as % of the group's own box so they scale.
- Water: two ripple sheets sliding against each other, the city mirrored at the waterline (`city-reflection.svg`: the mid buildings and their steady lights flipped, saved by draw_mid in `CITY_REFLECTION`), the reflected lights wobbling, the robot mirrored in the water (`robot-reflection.svg`, the same drawing flipped; drawn by draw_water in the mid layer via `WATER_EXTRAS`, so the duck, boats and periscope float over it) and rings spreading from its legs and the sword's tip.
- Lit windows: most stay on; the rest are split into three overlay files per layer (`*-lights-a/b/c.svg`) that switch on and off on different cycles (`wink`). People in the near windows pace about, each to its own rhythm (`walker.svg` in a clipped mover per window, `fx-walk`).
- Detection boxes: every object (the two giants included) has an invisible `.anchor` in its own layer or moving group, sized to it; the boxes themselves all sit in one hud layer on top of everything, so no art can cover a label. home.js finds the anchors under the pointer, takes the smallest (so the duck wins over the monster it floats in front of), and places that one box over the anchor; one box at a time, never unprompted (the user's rule). Labels are English; the gull's is "seagull eating a fry", the forge's "forge and fire". `Layer.det()` / a mover's `det()` register them; `write_dets()` writes the hud.
- `shared/home.css`: home styles. Layers move at their `data-depth` rate on scroll and mouse move (`home/home.js`; not on phones). One opening sequence (push-in, layers rise, lights on, the first punch and the monster's dizzy stars, detection boxes, the tanks fire), then the slow loops and the movers. `.scene` keeps the 1600:720 shape: `--scene` is its preferred share of the hero height, `--focus` the point kept centred when it is cropped (phones, tablets), `--top` the share kept clear for the title on short screens.
- `kids/<id>/games/*.html`: one self-contained game per file. Served at `<id>.tang.ninja/games/<slug>`.
- `kids/<id>/games.json`: optional per-game `slug`, `blurb`, `title`, `added` (YYYY-MM-DD; shows "New" for 14 days), order (first = shown first). Games missing from it are shown first.
- `kids/<id>/games/<same name>.jpg|png`: optional cover image, 960x600.
- `site.json`: domain and the kids: `id` (subdomain), `name`, `theme` (`hazard`, `comic` or `dossier`, in `shared/themes/`; fonts per theme are in build.mjs `THEMES`).
- `templates/kid.html` + `shared/kid.css`: the games-page structure shared by every theme.
- `dist/_worker.js` (generated) routes `<id>.tang.ninja` to `dist/<id>/`; `wrangler.jsonc` deploys it as the Worker (`run_worker_first`) with `dist/` as its assets (`dist/.assetsignore` keeps the worker file out of the assets).

## Adding or updating a game
1. Copy the `.html` into `kids/<id>/games/`, keeping the original file name so later versions overwrite it.
2. Add an entry at the top of that kid's `games.json`: a short `slug`, `added` date, and a one-sentence `blurb` written from the game's own title-screen text.
3. Make a cover: `node tools/thumbs.cjs kids/<id>/games/<file>.html`, then look at the image.
4. `node build.mjs`, check the output, commit and push.

## Home page data
- Citation numbers and the per-year bars come from Google Scholar (user Eo3rkZcAAAAJ); the date in the figure caption says when. Refresh them when asked.
- Papers are grouped by year, with Y Tang in bold and `data-area` p/r/h (photogrammetry, remote sensing, hyperspectral) for the topic filter. Award badges come from the CV.
- Honors, education, experience and service come from the user's CV (October 2026). GPAs and course credits are left out. The award cards carry a selectivity stat (top 0.04%, 2 of 4,812; top 1%; 1 of 57 and so on): the ratios are the CV's, the Talbert Abrams "No. 2 of the year's PE&RS papers" is from ASPRS's own award-winners page (the CV calls it the first honorable mention).
- Service lists the journals in order of impact factor with the IF on each card: Journal Citation Reports 2025 (Clarivate, released June 2026), taken from the publishers' pages (MDPI, Frontiers) and the JCR mirrors wos-journal.info and bioxbio.com where the publisher's page could not be read. CVPR is shown as No. 2 of all publications in Google Scholar Metrics 2025. Refresh the IFs each July when the new JCR is out.

## Rules
- Only publish facts about the user that they gave, that are on their Google Scholar profile, or that are in their CV.
- Never put the kids' ages or other personal details on the site.
- The CV is written on the page. No CV file or download link (the user's choice).
- Work and family stay apart: the home page does not link to the kids' pages, and their pages do not link back to it.
- The hero uses original characters (a survey robot and a cyclops monster). The user loves Godzilla, Ultraman, EVA, Kamen Rider and Berserk, but existing characters like those are not drawn on the site; keep new art original.
- Mozi's `Spiderman` Vite project (Marvel character; its own README forbids redistribution) is deliberately not on the site. Ask before adding it.
